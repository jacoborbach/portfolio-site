The situation

I own six web properties. Two of them had genuine authority: ghostchat.dev at Domain Rating 30 and gmailytics.com at DR 22, both earned through real product content. The other four — my portfolio, kaspawoo.com, hummustrail.com, and theabroadbible.com — were sitting at or near zero.

Worse, this portfolio site showed 289 referring domains and still had Domain Rating 0. That number is the first useful lesson in the whole project.

Diagnosis: referring domains are not authority

Nearly all 289 of those referring domains were scraper junk — automated aggregators, parked domains, and directory spam that accumulate on any site that exists for long enough. They are counted, they look impressive in a report, and they are worth approximately nothing.

This is the single most common way SEO reporting misleads founders. "We grew your backlinks by 200%" can be completely true and completely meaningless at the same time. A handful of genuine links from sites with real authority outperforms hundreds of junk domains, and no amount of the latter substitutes for the former.

The intervention

Rather than chase external links from a standing start, I built a deliberate hub-and-spoke graph out of the authority I already owned. Shipped 16 July 2026:

  • The two high-authority SaaS sites link to the portfolio hub from their About pages and footers, via genuine founder attribution
  • The hub links out to all five other properties from real project cards, not a link dump
  • The two travel sites cross-link as sister publications
  • Every link dofollow — nofollow links are excluded from Domain Rating entirely, so a nofollow plan would have produced nothing
  • Every link verified in the server-rendered HTML with curl, not just in the browser — client-rendered links are a common silent failure
  • Attribution UTMs on inbound links, stripped from the address bar after analytics capture so they never create duplicate-URL problems

Two findings worth more than the numbers themselves:

The source sites went up too

Because the hub links back to all five properties including the two donors, the graph is a closed loop rather than a one-way funnel. Authority circulates instead of draining. Both source sites gained despite being the ones "giving" links.

The exchange rate is measurable

A DR 13 hub spread across five outbound links buys each downstream site roughly DR 3–5. Both travel sites landed on exactly DR 3 despite having wildly different referring-domain counts (289 versus 105) — which is direct confirmation that the junk domains contributed nothing and the single hub link did all the work.

A second fix: keyword cannibalization

Authority alone doesn't rank pages. While reviewing Search Console for Hummus Trail, I found a textbook cannibalization: the homepage was ranking around position 3 for "hummus trail map" and getting zero clicks, because it embedded the same interactive map as the dedicated /the-route page — which was stuck at position 7. Two of my own pages were competing, and the wrong one was winning.

The fix, deployed 20 July 2026:

  • Rewrote the dedicated page's title, meta description, and H1 to lead with the exact target phrase
  • Re-anchored every internal link pointing at it — navigation, footers, the homepage SEO block, and the machine-readable llms.txt — from the generic "The Route" to the descriptive "Hummus Trail Map"

Anchor text is how you tell Google which of your pages owns a query. Generic anchors like "read more" or "the route" waste that signal entirely. This costs nothing but attention, and it's the kind of thing an audit PDF flags and nobody ever implements.

What this doesn't prove

Internal linking is plumbing, not demand. It moved every site off zero, but it has a ceiling — and I hit it. Going higher now requires genuine external links: editorial placements, journalist citations, guest posts, and data-led pages worth referencing.

Of the six sites, only the two SaaS properties currently carry meaningful organic traffic, and they earned it with content depth rather than link structure. The travel sites have authority and rank in the top ten for their core cluster, but converting that into real traffic is a content problem measured in quarters. The cannibalization fix is still in its measurement window. I'll update this page with the outcome either way.

Why this is the case study I show clients

Because it's the whole loop. I identified the problem in the data, designed the intervention, wrote the code, verified it at the HTTP level, measured it against dated baselines, found the ceiling, and said so out loud.

That's what you're actually hiring when you hire someone for organic growth — not a report, and not a promise about page one in thirty days.
