The Arc

What "everything" actually includes

  1. Scope — deciding what not to build The most valuable week of any product build. Most MVPs fail from being too big, not too small. I'll push back on features that don't earn their place, including ones you're attached to.
  2. Build the product Frontend, backend, database, infrastructure. Architected so that version two doesn't require throwing away version one.
  3. Landing pages that can multiply Built as templates from day one, because you'll want twenty of them later and you don't want each to be a ticket. This is where the build and the growth plan meet.
  4. Billing Stripe subscriptions, plans, upgrades, failed-payment recovery. The plumbing that turns users into revenue, done properly the first time.
  5. Launch with measurement Analytics, Search Console, conversion events, and error tracking wired in before launch — not bolted on after you realize you can't answer basic questions.
  6. Iterate over quarters New pages, new features, fixing what underperforms. Brands compound; launches don't. This is the phase most agencies aren't around for.

Proof

Three products, taken the whole way

Each of these was scoped, designed, built, launched, and grown by one person. They are live and you can use them right now.

GhostChat — SaaS with live paying customers

A privacy-first chat widget with a ~15KB SDK, up to 20× smaller and 3–8× faster than Intercom or Drift. Cloudflare Workers, Durable Objects, Supabase, Stripe billing, an AI responder running on Claude in production, a WordPress plugin, and an open-source agent runner on npm. Idea to paying customers, solo. ghostchat.dev

Gmailytics — 5.2M+ emails cleaned

A web app and Chrome extension that bulk-unsubscribes you from inbox clutter. It has cleaned over 4.5 million emails and freed 300GB of storage, and it's Google CASA Tier 2 certified — an independent third-party security assessment that most solo products never attempt, let alone pass. gmailytics.com

KaspaWoo — product plus open-source distribution

A cryptocurrency payment gateway for WooCommerce, released open source with its own npm package underneath it. A different launch shape: the product is the distribution, and the plugin ecosystem is the channel. kaspawoo.com

And the growth half. Building the product is only the first act. All of these sit inside an organic growth strategy I designed and executed myself. That combination is the actual offer here: someone who ships the product and knows how anyone will ever find it.
